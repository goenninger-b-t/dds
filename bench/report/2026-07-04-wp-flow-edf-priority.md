## (1) EDF vs round-robin — deadline misses (mixed LATENCY_BUDGET, saturated)

3 writers, budgets 10.0/2.5/1.5 ms, 20 samples each, 1 ms service, W simultaneous arrivals per batch (load 1.0, EDF-feasible). A sample MISSES if completion > arrival+budget. The tightest-budget writer is registered LAST, so round-robin's budget-blind rotation reaches it late — EDF reorders by deadline.

| policy | served | deadline misses | miss rate | total lateness (ms) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-----------|---------------------|
| **:edf** | 60 | 0 | 0.0% | 0.00 |
| round-robin | 60 | 20 | 33.3% | 30.00 |

**EDF misses 0 vs round-robin 20** — EDF orders by deadline, so the tight-budget stream is serviced first and the aggregate miss count is no worse (typically materially better) than budget-blind RR. This is the FR-QOS ordering benefit of the LATENCY_BUDGET-anchored policy.

## (2) :priority vs round-robin — service share + starvation bound

3 writers, TRANSPORT_PRIORITY 10/5/1, saturated (all always pending), 300 turns, aging quantum 10000000 ns / 1000000 ns service. MAX-GAP = longest run of consecutive turns a writer went UNSERVED (its observed starvation bound).

| writer (priority) | :priority served | :priority max-gap | round-robin served | round-robin max-gap |
|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| prio 10 | 292 | 1 | 100 | 2 |
| prio 5 | 5 | 50 | 100 | 2 |
| prio 1 | 3 | 90 | 100 | 2 |

The high-priority writer (prio 10) takes the LION'S SHARE under `:priority` (292/300 turns vs RR's fair 100) — the FR-QOS TRANSPORT_PRIORITY benefit. The low-priority writer (prio 1) is FAVOURED LAST yet its max-gap is BOUNDED at 90 turns (aging lifts its effective priority until it wins) — NOT the unbounded starvation a pure highest-first policy would inflict. RR is priority-blind (every writer's gap ~ W-1 = 2).

## Honest framing (FR-LANG-7)

- Selection is ORTHOGONAL to pacing: `:edf`/`:priority` change only WHICH writer drains next, never the byte rate (all three policies shape to the same aggregate rate) or the wire bytes (ADR 0016).
- EDF is keyed on **LATENCY_BUDGET**, not QoS DEADLINE (which is the periodicity/liveliness contract here); LATENCY_BUDGET is a max-delay HINT informing ORDERING, so a saturated bucket may still miss — EDF minimises misses, it does not guarantee zero.
- `:priority` favours high-priority writers but AGES pending writers so low-priority progress is bounded (quantum 10000000 ns); it is priority-WITH-fairness, not strict priority.
